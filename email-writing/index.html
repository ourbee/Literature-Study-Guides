PART ONE

The anatomy of a formal email

Subject line, salutation, body, and sign-off.

Anatomy · 1

The subject line

  • The first thing read — often the only thing read before a decision is made to open the mail.
  • Must be specific, not vague: "Leave Application — 3–15 August 2026", not "Leave".
  • Never left blank. A blank subject line reads as careless or, worse, suspicious.
  • Kept short enough to display in full in an inbox preview — under ten words is a safe rule of thumb.

Anatomy · 2

Salutation and register

Formal salutations

"Dear Dr. Sharma," "Respected Madam," "Dear Sir/Madam," when the recipient's name is not known.

Register

No contractions ("cannot", not "can't"). No emoji, no exclamation marks used for emphasis. Courtesy words — "kindly", "please", "I would be grateful" — carry the tone that punctuation cannot.

Anatomy · 3

The body: three movements

  • (text, 2) OPENING — state the purpose in the first line. Do not bury it.
  • (text, 2) DEVELOPMENT — one idea per paragraph; supporting detail, dates, attachments referenced here.
  • (text, 2) CLOSING — the action requested, and by when.
  • Paragraphs stay short: three to five lines is the working limit for a screen-read document.

Anatomy · 4

Sign-off and signature block

"Yours sincerely," or "Regards," followed by full name, designation, and institutional affiliation — never the first name alone in a formal register.

  • A signature block is not decoration; it is how the recipient verifies who is writing and in what capacity.
  • Contact details (phone, alternate email) belong here, not in the body.

PART TWO

Register, purpose, and practice

Formal against informal; the common types of professional email.

ENG-SEC-1-1 · Business WritingCreated by Ritwik Balo
Comparison

Formal email vs. informal message

Formal Email

Full sentences. "I am writing to inform you..." Proofread before sending. Reply expected within a working day.

Informal Message

Fragments and abbreviations. "Hey, quick q—" Sent and forgotten. Reply expected within minutes.

Practice

Common types of professional email

  • The request — leave application, permission, extension of deadline.
  • The inquiry — asking for information not otherwise available.
  • The complaint — factual, unemotional, evidence-led.
  • The follow-up — polite, brief, references the original mail by date.
  • The cover email — accompanies an attached document (CV, report, assignment).
ENG-SEC-1-1 · Business WritingCreated by Ritwik Balo
Practice

Netiquette: CC, BCC, and tone

  • CC (carbon copy) — visible to all; used when a party needs to be informed, not acted upon.
  • BCC (blind carbon copy) — invisible to other recipients; used for mass mail or discretion.
  • Reply-all is used only when every recipient genuinely needs the reply.
  • Proofread before sending — an email cannot be unsent, and an error in it is a permanent record.
